Teaching

Approach

I teach data science and AI to students across a wide range of backgrounds, from computer science majors to students taking their first and only technical course. My courses emphasize hands-on practice with real datasets, plain language over jargon, and the habit of asking what a result actually means before reporting it. I care as much about how students reason with data and AI tools as about the tools themselves.

Courses at Tennessee Tech

CSC 3220 — Fundamentals of Data Science

Fall 2026

An undergraduate introduction to the full data science workflow: collecting and cleaning data, exploratory analysis, visualization, and a first look at statistical modeling and machine learning. Students work hands-on in R throughout the semester and finish with a project on a dataset of their choice.

CSC 2220 — Data Science and AI for Everyone

Fall 2026

A general education course with no prerequisites, open to students from any major. We look at where data comes from, how AI systems are built and where they fail, and how to evaluate claims made with data in the news and at work. Labs use visual, no-code tools so that students can build and inspect models without writing code.

Mentoring

I supervise graduate teaching assistants and serve on master's thesis committees in the Department of Computer Science. I am happy to hear from students interested in human-centered AI, explainable AI, or digital health, whether for a thesis, an independent study, or an undergraduate research project. The best way to start is an email describing what you have worked on and what you would like to learn.
